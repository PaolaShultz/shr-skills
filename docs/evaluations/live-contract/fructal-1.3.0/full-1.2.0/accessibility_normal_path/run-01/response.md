Make the consequence preview part of the approval step itself. The fixture records `state=blocked`. The keyboard barrier and missing failure announcement come from your report. This is a proposed redesign. I made no changes.

1. **Review.** A keyboard-focusable **Review approval** button opens a dialog and moves focus to its heading. Show the action, its target, and its consequences as readable text before the **Approve** and **Cancel** buttons. Screen-reader users can read the preview in order, and keyboard users can scroll it and reach both buttons. Opening the dialog leaves approval pending.
2. **Decide.** Only **Approve** submits. Before submission, **Cancel** or Escape closes the dialog, preserves entered work, and returns focus to **Review approval**. On submission, announce “Submitting approval” and prevent duplicate submissions. On confirmed success, close the dialog, focus the approved item, and announce the result.
3. **Recover.** On confirmed failure, keep the preview and entered work in place. Show a persistent message beside the recovery control and announce it through an alert, for example, “Approval failed. Your changes are saved. Try again.” Use that wording only if the changes are retained. If submission is interrupted and the outcome is unknown, show “Checking approval status.” The system should reconcile the original request before offering retry, including when the user returns later.

**Verification plan.** Using only the keyboard, open and scroll a long preview, traverse controls in both directions, cancel, and reopen. Check focus placement and preservation of entered work. Repeat with a screen reader and confirm the consequences are readable before approval and that pending, failure, and success messages are announced without searching for them. Force a failure and retry, then interrupt a submission and reopen it. Verify that the displayed status matches the recorded outcome and that repeated activation produces at most one approval.
