Give each customer submission one durable request ID. Email, the dashboard, and support should use that ID to answer the same three questions: **What has happened, who owns the next step, and what can the customer safely do now?** Resolve disagreements according to which actor can attest to the disputed fact.

The fixture contains only `state=blocked`. The conflicting statuses, duplicate requests, and unclear handoffs are reported problems. The snapshot gives no cause, owner, or billing outcome. Audit, privacy, and approval are supplied constraints. The exact approval scope, retention rules, and access permissions remain open. The following is a proposed design. No changes or external requests were made.

### Proposed workflow

1. **Accept one submission.** Before confirmation, show the customer what they are requesting, any billing consequence, and when it can occur. Bind that confirmation to a submission key. Atomically record the key, the confirmed request version, and its request ID. If the acknowledgement is lost, a retry with the same key and content returns the original ID and current status. Changed content requires an explicit amendment or new submission. Support can look up the original on the customer’s behalf within its existing permissions.

2. **Assign authority by fact.** The request record owns receipt, confirmed version, and handoff history. The approval service records whether the applicable gate has passed for that version. The compliance reviewer owns any required review decision and its restricted rationale. Billing owns the outcome of a billing operation. Support owns customer-facing recovery when assigned and can report discrepancies. Each actor can update only the facts it owns. A missing or conflicting decision keeps the governed effect on hold.

3. **Make handoff acceptance explicit.** Record the current owner, intended recipient, next action, and a handoff ID together. The sending owner remains accountable until the recipient accepts. Acceptance transfers ownership once, even if the acknowledgement is retried. An overdue or rejected handoff returns to the sending owner’s work queue with a recovery action. The customer sees a role and a meaningful next step, such as “Awaiting compliance review. No action needed from you.” If the reviewer needs a correction, direct the customer to the relevant field and preserve permitted entries. Re-evaluate approvals affected by the change.

4. **Publish status from recorded events.** Build the dashboard and support view from the same ordered request history, filtered by role. Send email only after the corresponding event is recorded. Label each email with its effective time and a link to current status, so a delayed message remains intelligible. Keep restricted reasons and sensitive inputs in their authorized store. Record attributable decisions, versions, transfers, and effect references under the applicable audit and retention rules. For an unexplained `blocked` state, show “Status needs investigation,” assign support to reconcile the source records, and give the customer a follow-up path without asking them to submit again.

5. **Reconcile billing and cancellation.** Release a governed billing instruction only after the required decision covers the confirmed version. Give the operation a stable billing reference. After a timeout, show “Billing outcome pending” and make billing responsible for lookup by that reference. Replay only when the outcome is established as safe to retry or billing guarantees idempotent replay. For cancellation, record receipt and serialize it against release. If cancellation wins and policy permits it, suppress release. If an instruction is already in flight, show “Cancellation requested” while billing reconciles the effect. Report cancellation, charge, or reversal only when the responsible system confirms it.

### Verification plan

| Exercise | Evidence required before adoption |
|---|---|
| Complete a request through all five actors | One ID links the confirmed intent, accepted handoffs, applicable decision, and billing outcome. No governed effect precedes approval. |
| Double-submit, lose the acknowledgement, then retry through support | Concurrent retries resolve to one request. A separate intentional submission remains possible. Changed content cannot silently inherit the earlier confirmation or approval. |
| Drop a handoff acknowledgement, reject a transfer, and deliver email events out of order | Exactly one owner can act at each point. Overdue work has a recovery owner. An old email identifies its effective time, and its link opens the latest permitted status. |
| Interrupt review or reauthentication, then resume using a keyboard and screen reader | The authorized actor returns to the same request and relevant field. Permitted entries survive, focus is predictable, status changes are announced, and recovery works without a pointer. |
| Lose a billing response and race cancellation against release | A completed charge is discovered before replay. An unresolved outcome stays pending with billing. Both cancellation orderings produce an attributable result and at most one charge. |
| Inspect audit history and compare unrelated records before and after | An authorized reviewer can reconstruct decisions and ownership. Email, customer views, logs, and support views disclose only permitted information. Other requests, account permissions, and unrelated balances retain their prior values. |

Before adoption, the approval and compliance owners need to confirm which effects require which decisions, what may persist during interruption, and who may see or retain each field. Billing needs to confirm its lookup, retry, and cancellation guarantees. These checks are planned; the fixture contains no executable workflow or actor acceptance evidence.
