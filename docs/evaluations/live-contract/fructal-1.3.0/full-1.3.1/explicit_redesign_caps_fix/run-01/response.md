**Proposed redesign.** Give the requester a visible `awaiting_approval` state and the approver a specific decision to make. The required approval comes from your request. The fixture shows only `state=blocked`, so it establishes neither who owns the decision nor whether an approval request already exists. [workflow.txt](./workflow.txt)

1. **Submit for approval.** Show the requester what they are asking to authorize. On submission, save the work and its current position, bind the approval request to that version, and return a durable request ID. Show who owns the next step. If no approver can be assigned, show a routing error beside the request and give the workflow owner a way to assign it.
2. **Decide and resume.** The authorized approver opens the same request and approves or declines the specified version. Approval makes that version eligible for the protected step. The requester resumes at the saved position and can see what proceeding will do. A decline returns the work to the requester with the reason and a revision path. A material edit requires approval of the revised version.
3. **Recover safely.** After an interruption or timeout, reopen the request by ID and check its recorded status before retrying. Repeated submissions or decisions resolve to one recorded result. A requester can withdraw a pending request and retain the draft. If withdrawal and approval race, record one transition and show both actors the outcome.

**Verification plan.** Exercise approval, decline, revision, withdrawal, and missing-approver routing. Interrupt submission and decision recording, then retry and confirm both actors see the same ID, owner, and outcome. Confirm the protected step stays gated for pending, declined, withdrawn, and superseded versions. Check that saved input, position, and unrelated records survive each path. Using a keyboard and screen reader, verify that each actor can identify the current status, reach their next action, and recover from an error.
